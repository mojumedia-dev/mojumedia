## WGU at a glance

- **Founded:** 1997, by 19 U.S. governors.
- **Model:** Nonprofit, online, competency-based education. All bachelor's and master's degrees are competency-based (not cohort-based). First university to make that shift end-to-end.
- **Mission (their words):** "To change lives for the better by creating pathways to opportunity."
- **Positioning:** "Talent is universal, even if opportunity isn't." Barriers WGU explicitly targets: affordability, location, time.
- **Scale:** ~150,000+ students. Serious edtech operation.
- **Founding thesis:** internet as access equalizer + lower-cost model + competency-based over seat-time. Those three ideas are still the north star.
- **Leadership:** Scott Pulsipher is president/CEO. Frames the org as mission-led.

**Why this matters for the interview:** WGU sells mission first, comp second. Every candidate they push forward believes in the mission. Don't perform it — actually believe it (which is easy: online, affordable, outcomes-focused higher-ed access is a genuinely defensible mission). Reference "changing lives" and "expanding access" in your own words at least once during the call.

## The Role Decoded

**Senior Software Product Manager, Technical Grade 410.** Individual contributor (not people-manager) role but with mentorship responsibilities for less-senior PMs. Sits on a cross-functional squad with design, engineering, and business stakeholders. Reports up through a Senior Manager / Director of Product.

**What you'd own:**
- End-to-end user experience for a specific product area (they don't name which one in the posting — likely student-facing platform, faculty/mentor tooling, or a data/analytics surface).
- Strategic roadmap for that area, with business cases + measurable outcomes.
- Product discovery: interviews, focus groups, surveys, usability sessions, online community listening.
- Product delivery: writing epics + stories, prioritizing, working with Scrum teams, UAT coordination.
- Stakeholder alignment: presenting roadmaps to leadership, managing expectations when things slip.
- Occasional RFI/RFP vendor selection.

**KSAs they're explicitly screening for:**
1. Zeros in on the best strategy for a business outcome (not just tasks).
2. Storyteller — can explain WHY and get buy-in.
3. Multiple product discovery techniques on the shelf.
4. Redirects "let's build X" conversations back to problem + outcome first.
5. Trade-off decisions grounded in business goals + market + user needs.
6. Uses data to prioritize, monitor product health, measure success.
7. Handles change gracefully.
8. Strong writing + presentation.
9. Empathy for users.
10. Positive team-building instincts.

**Comp math:** Grade Technical 410 range posted is $143.7k–$222.7k. Utah senior PM average per Indeed sits around $143.6k (basically the floor of the WGU range). Levels.fyi shows WGU PM total comp around $96k–$135k for a straight PM, higher for senior. Realistic hire point for a senior IC with your years of experience is probably $160k–$185k. Anchor higher than the floor when Mallory asks about expectations.

**Benefits (from the posting):** bonuses, medical/dental/vision/mental health, HSA + FSA, life + disability + accident/critical illness/hospital indemnity coverage, legal + ID theft, retirement plan, discounted WGU tuition (nice perk if you or family want to enroll), flexible PTO with no accrual, flexible sick, 11 paid holidays, up to 12 weeks parental leave.

**Travel:** Up to 20%, 1–2 company summits per year.

**Minimum requirements:**
- Bachelor's degree.
- 7 years as a Software PM / Product Owner or related.
- Professional product certification (CSPO, Pragmatic Marketing, etc.).
- Agile environment experience.

**Preferred:**

**Requirement flags to think about ahead of time:**

Options: acknowledge you don't and cite equivalent-experience clause (they'll substitute 1 year experience for 1 year education/cert), OR commit to obtaining CSPO between offer and start (2-day course + exam, common ask).
- **7 years Software PM experience.** Framing question: does your Mojumedia + client-product portfolio work count? Absolutely, if you tell the story right. MRS Hobby SO catalog + refresh pipeline, Water For Life React/Vite app with the Rife frequency guide + PDF export, Regal CRM Audit dashboard product, MojuCrew productized SaaS. ## WGU's Hiring Process (from jobs.wgu.edu/how-we-hire)

Five stages, ~3–5 weeks end to end:

1. **Talent Partner Conversation** — where you are Aug 20. Video call, ~30 min. Recruiter learns your background. You learn about WGU + role. Screens for fit + mission alignment.
2. **Hiring Leader Interview** — 45–60 min with the leader who owns the role. Deep dive on your experience, behavioral patterns, problem-solving approach. This is the real decision-maker interview.
3. **Team Interviews** — customized panel round. For a Senior PM role, expect: cross-functional panel (design + engineering + peer PM + stakeholder), plus possibly a written assessment or scenario-based case (product discovery for a hypothetical WGU problem).
4. **Offer** — recruiter walks you through Total Rewards, timelines, next steps.
5. **Background Check** — standard, contingent on offer.

**Cultural framing they published:** "Know the Mission" and "Be Yourself." Perfection is not the bar; authenticity + fit is.

## Positioning Strategy — the agency / product-owner tension

**The elephant:** You run an agency AND you're applying for a full-time W2 role. Interviewers will wonder: is he actually going to shut down Mojumedia? Will he moonlight? Is this a placeholder while he waits for something better?

**Don't dodge, address it:**

> "I've been running Mojumedia for a while now, and it's given me a wide angle on product work — I've built for eight different verticals in the last two years alone. What that experience clarified for me is that I do my best work when I'm deep in ONE product, iterating on ONE user population, watching a roadmap play out over multiple quarters. Agency work rewards the opposite: shallow-and-broad. That's why WGU appealed to me. I'm ready to trade breadth for depth, and I'd be winding Mojumedia down or handing the client book off to another agency the moment WGU says yes."

Practice that until it lands naturally. If Mallory pokes further, be honest about your intended path (wind down client-by-client over 30–60 days after signing).

**Turn the agency into an asset, not a liability:**
- 8+ verticals means you've seen how to translate business problems into product problems, over and over.
- Client work means you've had to be persuasive with stakeholders who could fire you at will — sharpens the "influence without authority" muscle every PM needs.
- Owning a SaaS product (MojuCrew) means you've felt the pricing / positioning / retention pain from the inside, not just theoretically.

## STAR stories to prep (map to KSAs)

Prep 4–6 stories in STAR format (Situation, Task, Action, Result). WGU's behavioral interviews at Stage 2 will lean on these heavily. Common WGU behavioral prompt reported by past candidates: "Tell me about a time you had to confront a stakeholder about the project."

**Story 1: Data-driven product decision that changed direction**
- Situation: [Regal CRM Audit dashboard, 5-tab restructure per Tonya's ask]
- Task: Determine whether to invest in the restructure vs. keep incremental improvements
- Action: Analyzed usage patterns of existing tabs, interviewed the three stakeholders (Marissa/Tonya/Heather) about what they actually looked at, ran the numbers on time-to-answer for common questions
- Result: Shipped 5-tab restructure that measurably reduced stakeholder ping-me-a-question frequency; stakeholders reported the dashboard was now their first-open browser tab
- KSA mapped: uses data to prioritize + assess product health

**Story 2: Trade-off decision that killed a feature**
- Situation: Water For Life React app frequency guide feature
- Task: Owner wanted dynamic AI-generated safety notes per condition; you had already scoped it
- Action: Pushed back with a compliance framing (FDA Class 1 device, LLM safety notes = real risk), proposed a general-disclaimer + owner-curated content model instead, walked owner through the tradeoff
- Result: Owner (Doug) accepted the tradeoff, MojuMedia shipped a defensible product-sense trade
- KSA mapped: trade-off decisions grounded in business goals + user needs; redirects "let's build X" to problem + outcome

**Story 3: Confronting a stakeholder (they've asked this one at WGU per Glassdoor)**
- Situation: [Doug wanted AI-generated ailment-specific safety notes for the Rife frequency app; you knew that was a legal/UX risk]
- Task: Deliver hard news to a client who was excited about the feature
- Action: Prepared the compliance context, showed him the MCAS-sheet example that would have LLM-hallucinated a wrong warning, offered an alternative
- Result: Client changed direction to owner-curated + general disclaimer; relationship stayed warm
- KSA mapped: handles change / influence without authority / storytelling

**Story 4: Discovery technique that surfaced a non-obvious problem**
- Situation: [MRS Hobby SO catalog build — client wanted every Bandai SKU, you noticed the distributor inventory was misaligned]
- Task: Figure out whether the catalog approach was right
- Action: Ran an inventory-vs-catalog cross-reference, discovered 40% of proposed SKUs had zero distributor availability
- Result: Redirected the catalog scope to only "distributor-stocked" SKUs, saved months of dead-inventory listings
- KSA mapped: expertise in product discovery, look beyond the obvious

**Story 5: Roadmap communication under pressure**
- Situation: [Regal CRM board review this coming week; you delivered a full QA report + remediation plan]
- Task: Communicate current state, risks, and next steps to non-technical stakeholders
- Action: Wrote a PASS/WARN/FAIL structured report with board-ready summary at top; distilled 3 concrete FAILs from a much bigger findings list; provided cost estimates and next actions
- Result: Two of the three FAILs closed within hours; stakeholders had a clear document to reference in-meeting
- KSA mapped: storytelling, roadmap presentation, distills clarity from ambiguity

**Story 6: Mentoring a less-senior teammate**
- (You may need to reach for this one. If you don't have a clean "mentored a PM" story, pivot to mentoring a client's internal team on product process, or mentoring a junior designer on your team.)

## Product-sense case questions to prep

Not for the Aug 20 call, but you'll almost certainly hit these at Stage 3. Given WGU's context, expect prompts like:

- **"Design a feature that helps a WGU student decide when to attempt a competency assessment."** (Product sense + user empathy for adult learners with jobs and families.)
- **"How would you improve the mentor-student communication experience for at-risk students?"** (User empathy + prioritization.)
- **"WGU's completion rate for a specific program is dropping. Walk me through how you'd diagnose the problem and prioritize solutions."** (Data-driven + product discovery + business framing.)
- **"Which metric would you use to measure the success of a new student onboarding flow, and why?"** (Metrics selection.)

Practice one out loud before Stage 3. The framework: Clarify the problem → identify the user + their goal → propose 3 solution directions → pick one with reasoning → define the success metric → discuss risks.

## Questions YOU should ask Mallory (Aug 20)

Prepare 4–5, use 2–3 depending on time. Order matters — start with questions that show you've done homework, end with logistics.

1. **"What does the org structure around this role look like? Which PM group is this sitting inside, and how many other PMs are on the team?"** (Understands you'll want peer collaboration.)
2. **"What are the most important outcomes the hiring manager wants this Senior PM to drive in year one?"** (Signals results orientation.)
3. **"How is WGU balancing the competency-based model with the newer online-learning trends around AI-assisted tutoring and adaptive assessment? Is this role likely to touch that work?"** (Shows you've done edtech research + you're forward-looking.)
4. **"What does a good week look like for this role once someone's ramped up?"** (Practical, shows you're thinking about the job.)
5. **"What's the interview flow after this call? Who would I meet next, and roughly when?"** (Sets expectations + signals interest.)
6. **"How does WGU think about senior IC growth vs. management track for PMs at this grade?"** (Career trajectory signal.)

Don't ask about salary if she doesn't bring it up first — but be ready if she does.
